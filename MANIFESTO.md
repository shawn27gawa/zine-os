# ZineOS Manifesto

Zines are personal.

They can be rough, experimental, unfinished, strange, beautiful, political, quiet, chaotic, or deeply ordinary.

The tools used to make them should not decide what they become.

## Creativity belongs to the creator

ZineOS does not exist to automate creativity.

It exists to remove the friction around creativity.

The creator chooses the story.
The creator chooses the images.
The creator chooses what stays, what disappears, and what feels right.

Software should assist those decisions, not replace them.

## Editing is a craft

A zine is more than a collection of pages.

Sequence matters.
Rhythm matters.
Whitespace matters.
The relationship between an image and the page beside it matters.

ZineOS treats editing as a creative act.

Its purpose is not simply to generate layouts, but to help creators think about how a publication is experienced from beginning to end.

## Tools should get out of the way

Making a zine should not require mastering expensive or complicated software.

Creative energy should be spent on the work itself, not fighting the interface.

ZineOS aims to make the technical parts of publishing simpler, more understandable, and more accessible.

## Open by default

A publication should not be trapped inside a single application.

Projects should remain readable, editable, portable, and understandable.

ZineOS uses open formats whenever possible and keeps the underlying structure visible to the creator.

Your work belongs to you.

## AI is an assistant, not an author

AI can organize.
AI can compare.
AI can suggest.
AI can review.

But AI should not silently make creative decisions on behalf of the creator.

Every meaningful decision should remain inspectable, editable, and reversible.

The human remains the editor in chief.

## Paper matters

ZineOS embraces digital publishing, but it begins with the physical object.

Pages have dimensions.
Paper has texture.
Spreads have relationships.
Printing creates constraints.

Those constraints are not bugs.

They are part of publishing.

## Imperfection is allowed

Not every page needs to be optimized.

Not every photograph needs to be perfect.

Not every decision needs to follow a template.

A handmade mark, an awkward crop, an empty page, or a broken rule may carry more meaning than a technically perfect layout.

ZineOS should create room for those decisions.

## Build systems, not styles

ZineOS should never impose a single visual language.

It provides blocks, structures, workflows, and tools that creators can combine, modify, ignore, or replace.

The system should adapt to the publication.

The publication should never have to adapt to the system.

## Our principle

**We don't automate creativity. We automate repetition.**

ZineOS exists so that more of the creator's time can be spent deciding what a publication should become.

Everything should remain editable.
